# Coding-subspace-aware global downlink

Implement the finite offline business optimizer. Input {switch_cooldown,packets,contacts} is valid and finite. There are 1..5 packets {id,dimension,priority,craft,band,release,deadline,requires}; dimension2..4, priority1..9, integer0<=release<deadline<=6. requires is a DAG of existing packet IDs. Each of1..10 contacts is {id,station,craft,band,group,start,end,rate,symbols}, integer0<=start<end<=6, rate1..2. symbols is a nonempty list of distinct {packet,vector} offers; it references matching craft/band packets and integer1<=vector<2**dimension. All names are opaque strings; each packet/contact ID is unique. switch_cooldown0..3. No malformed input or nonfinite number handling is requested.

A vector is a GF(2) coding coefficient bitmask. Receiving several vectors recovers the packet iff their binary linear rank equals dimension. Repeats and XOR-dependent symbols do NOT increase rank. A contact at integer tick start<=t<end may send any offered symbols, up to rate symbol rows total, including to different eligible packets. Unused capacity is allowed; the same offer may be sent again in later ticks or repeated in the same tick, but every row consumes one rate slot even if dependent. A packet is eligible only while NOT already decoded at tick start, during release<=t<deadline, and after ALL prerequisites were decoded. When the cumulative span first reaches full dimension at tick t, completion=t+1. Its children become eligible no earlier than tick t+1, regardless of spare capacity. Incomplete subspaces persist between all contacts and ticks. Output row order within a tick has no execution meaning.

A used contact sends at least one symbol. Used contacts at a tick must have distinct station, distinct craft and distinct interference group IDs. An unused contact reserves nothing. Each station remembers its last ACTUALLY USED (band,tick). A different-band contact is blocked when current_tick-last_tick<=switch_cooldown; same band is allowed. Idle ticks do not update history. Thus packet spans, future decoding/unlocking and productive-use cooldown must be planned jointly across the entire horizon.

Optimize lexicographically: maximize sum of priorities of FULLY decoded packets, then decoded packet count, then SUM OF FINAL BINARY RANKS across every packet. Rank counts innovative information, not the number of physical transmission rows. There is no further earliest-finish, ID, canonical-basis or shortest-schedule tie-break. Accept ANY globally optimal legal plan, including redundant transmissions if they remain legal and globally optimal. Mere received counts/ranks are not sufficient state: different same-rank subspaces can have different future innovation.

Return exactly {score,packets,contacts,transmissions}. score={priority,count,rank}. packets includes EVERY packet once as {id,rank,completion}, null completion if not decoded. contacts is the SET of used {tick,contact} rows. transmissions=[{tick,contact,packet,vector},...]. All output list orders are immaterial; no basis or extra proof certificate is required. The verifier independently replays YOUR symbols, computes rank, completion, prerequisite eligibility, station history and resource usage, and verifies the globally optimal score.

Public example: P has dimension2,priority1,craftA/bandx,release0,deadline2,no prerequisites; switch_cooldown0. Contact choose at tick0,rate1 offers P vector1(01) or2(10). Contact later at tick1,rate1 offers only P vector1. Both use station s,craft A,band x,group g. Send vector2 at tick0,vector1 at tick1: score {priority:1,count:1,rank:2},P completion2. Sending1 first leaves rank1 after receiving1 again; counting two received rows as two independent dimensions is incorrect.
