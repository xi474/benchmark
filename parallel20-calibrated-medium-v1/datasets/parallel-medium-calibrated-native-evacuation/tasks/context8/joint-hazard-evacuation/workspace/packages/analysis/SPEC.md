# Joint evacuation through a finite hazard grid

This is a synthetic integer planning exercise, not a real emergency plan. Input is {grid,spread_cost,fire_seeds,closures,evacuees,shelters,max_ticks}. grid is a rectangular array of strings with '.' traversable and '#' wall cells, at most5 rows and6 columns. spread_cost is a same-shaped positive integer matrix. fire_seeds lists {r,c,time}, with valid nonwall coordinates and nonnegative integer ignition times. closures lists {r,c,start,end}, intervals with BOTH endpoints inclusive, forbidding occupancy of that cell at those integer times. Overlapping closures combine by OR. All data is well formed and finite. max_ticks is an integer1..10.

evacuees has1–4 distinct {id,r,c,release,eligible_shelters} people, starting at nonwall cells, release in0..max_ticks. eligible_shelters is a nonempty set of valid shelter IDs represented by a list. shelters has1–4 distinct {id,r,c,opens,closes,capacity}, at valid nonwall cells, integer0<=opens<closes<=max_ticks+1, capacity1..4. Multiple people or shelters may share a cell; the rules below still apply. These are prescribed destinations/qualifications, not interchangeable anonymous flow. Input/list order imposes no selection priority.

Compute earliest fire ignition at EVERY grid cell from ANY seed using orthogonal moves: entering neighboring cell v costs spread_cost[v] ticks. The fire ignores road closures. Its arrival is the minimum weighted path time, with the seed's own time as start. Return null for walls or never-reached cells. A traveler may occupy a cell at integer time t only if it is traversable, not closed at t, and either never burns or t is STRICTLY less than its earliest fire arrival. This applies at the person's release cell/time too. Waiting occupies its cell and must satisfy the same rule; closures do not merely block movement into a cell.

Choose a subset of people to evacuate and plan their routes JOINTLY. A selected person enters at exactly release and its stated starting cell, then each tick waits or moves one orthogonal cell. It may finish at an eligible shelter only at opens<=arrival<closes and arrival<=max_ticks. It may pass through a shelter without finishing. A successful arrival permanently consumes one of that shelter's total capacity slots. Once assigned at arrival t, the person still occupies its arrival cell at time t, then disappears from the road network starting t+1. A person not selected for evacuation NEVER enters this planning network; represent it as unassigned, not a permanent obstacle at its starting cell.

At each integer time, no TWO selected active people may occupy the same cell, INCLUDING release and shelter-arrival cells. Also forbid an opposing swap along the same edge in one tick: A at u and B at v cannot simultaneously move u->v and v->u. Other simultaneous noncolliding motion is allowed, including directed rotation cycles of length3 or greater. An already completed person imposes no occupancy or edge restriction after its arrival tick. Shelter capacity is by shelter ID, but physical same-cell occupancy still applies even when two shelter IDs share that cell. A path cannot teleport, enter before release or wait past an unsafe boundary.

Optimize lexicographically: FIRST maximize the number of assigned people; THEN minimize the SUM of their absolute arrival times. Unassigned people add nothing to that sum. There is no ID, path, shelter or processing-order tie-break. Accept ANY globally optimal legal joint witness. Independent earliest paths followed by a collision check, nearest-first greedy capacity allocation, or anonymous flow that exchanges people's eligible destinations can miss the optimum.

Return exactly {max_saved,total_arrival,fire_arrival,evacuees,shelters}. fire_arrival is the grid-shaped matrix. Each output evacuee is {id,status,shelter,arrival,path}. For status 'assigned', path has one [r,c] per tick from its release THROUGH arrival inclusive; shelter/arrival identify its final acceptance. For status 'unassigned', shelter:null,arrival:null,path:[]. Include every input person exactly once. Output shelters has {id,used,capacity} for every input shelter. max_saved and total_arrival must agree with these routes and be jointly optimal. Person/shelter/list/map ordering is irrelevant; the checker replays YOUR paths and does not require the reference assignment or path.

Public example: grid ['....'], no fire/closures, max_ticks3, unit spread costs. near=(0,0), far=(0,3), each opens0,closes4,capacity1. A starts(0,1),release0,eligible_shelters[near,far]. B starts(0,0),release1,eligible_shelters[near]. Assign A to far with path [[0,1],[0,2],[0,3]],arrival2; B to near with path [[0,0]],arrival1. max_saved2,total_arrival3. Greedily taking near for A at time1 leaves B without a legal shelter. All fire_arrival entries are null.

Collision example: a three-cell single-row corridor, people start at opposite ends at release0 and each is eligible only for the opposite-end shelter. They cannot both pass through, even though both individual shortest paths have length2. A second row provides a side lane and can make both feasible. Conversely, four people on a2x2 cycle can each move one cell clockwise simultaneously in one tick: endpoints are distinct and no opposing edge swap occurs, so the4-cycle is legal.
